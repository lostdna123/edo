Our broth

Twenty-four hours.
One pot.

Gukbap — 국밥, literally “soup rice” — is Korea's everyday comfort meal. Ours starts with pork bones and a full day on the stove. Hours of rolling heat draw everything out of the bones until the broth turns milky, round and deeply savoury.

We keep it clean on purpose. The broth is seasoned lightly, the way it's served in Korea, so you can finish it exactly to your taste at the table.

How to eat gukbap

Finished at the table.
By you.

In Korea, gukbap arrives gently seasoned and every diner tunes their own bowl. Here's the ritual.

  1. 하나

    Taste it first

    Take a spoonful of broth on its own. It's gentle on purpose — that's your starting point.

  2. 둘

    Season your way

    Salt and pepper are on every table. Add a pinch at a time until it tastes right to you.

  3. 셋

    Rice into the soup

    Tip your rice into the bowl and let it soak up the broth. That's the “bap” in gukbap.

  4. 넷

    Kimchi on top. Repeat.

    Lay a piece of kimchi on each spoonful. Run out? Refills are free.
